STREAM Lab Sustainable Tools for Risk Evaluation and Climate-Water Modeling

Gehendra Kharel

Ph.D. · Associate Professor · Department of Environmental and Geological Sciences · Texas Christian University

I model how climate and land-use change reshape water systems (flood risk, reservoir supply, water quality, and contaminant transport), and build open tools that turn that science into decisions. Central to all of this work is my role as an educator, training students and aspiring researchers to tackle these complex environmental challenges.

What the lab does

Capabilities

At the STREAM Lab, we translate complex environmental data and science into applied, fundable, and billable solutions for utilities, agencies, consultancies, and research partners.

01 / Climate risk

Climate-risk & extreme-event modeling

Translating climate projections into actionable flood, drought, and water-supply risk, including non-stationary design standards for infrastructure.

Built, deployed, citable

Open decision tools

Not just papers. Working, browser-based tools, several with archived, citable releases (Zenodo DOIs), as evidence that the modeling reaches practitioners.

Web app · open source

PFAS Censoring-Aware Screening Tool (PFAS-CAST)

Screens PFAS occurrence while correctly handling left-censored (non-detect) laboratory data, so values below detection limits do not bias the risk picture or the screening decisions that follow.

Live link and DOI: to be added
Web app · open source

PFAS Reach-Bracketing Tool

Brackets likely PFAS concentrations along a river reach under data scarcity, a practical aid for screening contamination and framing monitoring and permit decisions.

Launch tool doi:10.5281/zenodo.18937739
Web app · open source

SWOT-AHP Analyzer

Combines SWOT analysis with the Analytic Hierarchy Process to weight and prioritize strategy under expert judgment, built for structured environmental and policy decision-making.

Launch tool doi:10.5281/zenodo.18991287

Selected work

Featured projects

A few representative efforts, framed by the decision they inform rather than the journal they appeared in.

Flagship project · open source

OpenDW-Risk

An open, reproducible framework for drinking-water contaminant risk. It brings censored monitoring data, screening, and uncertainty into one transparent workflow that agencies and researchers can audit, reuse, and extend.

View on GitHub
Eagle Mountain Lake, TX · 2026

Climate-stressed water supply for a DFW reservoir

Problem
How will a warming, more variable climate change inflows to a major regional water-supply reservoir?
Approach
SWAT model driven by downscaled CMIP6 daily climate, coupled with observed reservoir operations.
Result
Identified seasonal inflow shifts and rising hot-dry stress relevant to long-range supply planning.
Dallas-Fort Worth, TX · 2023

Climate-informed IDF curves for stormwater design

Problem
Standard rainfall design assumes a stationary climate. Is that still safe for DFW infrastructure?
Approach
Tested non-stationarity in precipitation intensity-duration-frequency curves across the metroplex.
Result
Evidence that flood-design standards should account for changing extremes.
Upper Trinity River, TX · under review

PFAS across an urban river network

Problem
Where do "forever chemicals" concentrate across a city's waterways, and what does monitoring data really tell us?
Approach
Censoring-aware sum-PFAS mapping with uncertainty propagation and discharge-permit context.
Result
A defensible spatial picture of PFAS risk to inform regulation and siting.
Village Creek, Everman, TX · under review

Green infrastructure for bacteria reduction

Problem
Can watershed-scale green infrastructure cut the storm-driven E. coli spikes that drive TMDL failures?
Approach
Tail-aware modeling with dependence-robust inference on bacteria extremes.
Result
Quantified reductions in the extreme events that matter most for compliance.
